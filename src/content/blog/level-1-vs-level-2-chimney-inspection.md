### Level 1 vs. Level 2 vs. Level 3 at a Glance

The three levels are defined in <a href="https://www.nfpa.org/211" rel="nofollow" target="_blank">NFPA 211</a>, the national standard for chimneys, fireplaces, and vents. Each level includes everything in the levels below it.

| | Level 1 | Level 2 | Level 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Best for | Unchanged system in routine use | A change, a sale, or possible damage | A suspected hidden hazard |
| Reaches | Readily accessible areas | Level 1 plus attics, crawlspaces, and basements | Levels 1 and 2 plus concealed areas |
| Inside the flue | Visual check of what's reachable | Video scan of every flue | Included |
| Camera | Not required | Required (video scan or equivalent) | Included |
| Invasive | No | No | Yes |

### What a Level 1 Inspection Covers

Level 1 covers what's readily accessible. That means parts you can reach without tools or ladders.

The technician verifies the chimney structure and flue are basically sound. They confirm the chimney is free of obstructions and combustible deposits, such as creosote, the tar-like byproduct of burning wood.

Level 1 also checks the basic appliance installation and the connection between the appliance and the chimney.

### What a Level 1 Can Miss

Level 1 can't show what's inside the flue. Cracks and gaps in its protective lining stay hidden.

It also doesn't reach concealed areas. Clearance problems and heat damage near the chimney can go unnoticed.

That's why Level 1 fits only when nothing about the system has changed.

### What a Level 2 Inspection Adds

Level 2 goes beyond a visual check. The technician opens access doors and panels using ordinary tools.

It also checks clearances to combustibles. These are the required gaps between the chimney and wood framing or other materials that can burn.

No demolition is involved. Nothing permanent is removed.

### Why the Camera Matters

A Level 2 has to examine the inside of the flue. That's what separates it from a Level 1.

It requires a video scan, also called a chimney camera inspection, or an equivalent method that shows the inside surfaces and joints. In practice, that means a camera. An inspection sold as a "Level 2" that never looks inside the flue doesn't meet the standard.

The camera shows the flue liner, the protective lining inside the flue. It should show the full circumference clearly enough to reveal cracks, joint separation, and deposits. The <a href="https://www.csia.org/inspections.htm" rel="nofollow" target="_blank">Chimney Safety Institute of America</a> describes each level's scope in more detail.

### Liner Types and What the Camera Finds

Different liners fail in different ways.

- Clay tile liners can crack.
- Stainless steel liners can corrode at their joints.
- Cast-in-place liners can develop voids.

The camera records each defect so it's documented, not guessed at.

### Liner Size and the Connected Appliance

A liner also has to suit the appliance connected to it. The inspector documents whether the size is correct.

A mismatch can affect how the appliance drafts.

### Exterior Masonry, Crown, and Cap

Level 2 gives the exterior a closer look. The technician checks for spalling brick, failed mortar joints, and separation between the chimney and the house.

Spalling is when the face of a brick flakes or breaks away.

The crown, the concrete slab at the top of the chimney, and the cap, the metal cover over the flue, are checked too. Damage to either lets water in. See [chimney cap installation](https://fortworthchimneysweep.com/chimney-cap-installation-fort-worth-tx/) if the cap needs replacing.

### Attics, Crawlspaces, and Chases

Level 2 follows the chimney through the house. Those hidden areas are where a chase, the framed enclosure around the chimney, can hide gaps against the framing.

Water staining on joists and deteriorated insulation near the flue show up there too. The technician also confirms the clearances to combustibles described above.

See [water stains on ceiling next to chimney](https://fortworthchimneysweep.com/water-stains-on-ceiling-next-to-chimney/) if you've already noticed staining inside the house.

### Multi-Flue Chimneys and Connected Appliances

A Level 2 covers every flue in the chimney. It also covers the appliances connected to them.

A chimney with two flues gets both scanned.

### Masonry Chimneys vs. Factory-Built Fireplaces

Inspection procedures differ by construction. The CSIA publishes separate procedures for masonry fireplaces, masonry chimneys, and factory-built fireplaces.

A masonry chimney is built on site from brick or block. A factory-built fireplace is a prefabricated metal unit with a metal flue.

Access to a factory-built unit can require common tools to open panels. Tell the company which type you have.

### What a Level 3 Involves

Level 3 goes further than Level 2. It reaches concealed parts of the chimney and flue by removing permanently attached parts of the chimney or building as needed.

A Level 3 isn't routine. It's recommended only when a Level 1 or Level 2 points to a hidden hazard that can't be evaluated any other way.

### Level 2 When Buying or Selling a Home in Fort Worth

For buyers, a Level 2 shows the real condition of the liner before closing. A hidden crack in the flue is a fire and carbon monoxide risk you'd otherwise inherit.

For sellers, it shows the chimney's condition before a buyer's inspector does. Problems found on your timeline are easier to handle than problems found during negotiation.

### Level 2 After a Storm, Hail, or Chimney Fire

A Level 1 can miss damage inside the flue after a fire or weather event. A Level 2 checks for it.

If a chimney fire is happening now, get everyone out and call 911 first. After a fire, don't use the fireplace again until a Level 2 has been done.

### Relining, New Appliances, and Fuel Changes

Changing what the chimney serves changes what it has to handle. Relining changes the flue's shape or material. A new appliance or fuel changes what the flue carries.

See [chimney relining](https://fortworthchimneysweep.com/chimney-relining-fort-worth-tx/) for that service. A Level 2 confirms the existing chimney can handle the change.

### What a Proper Inspection Report Includes

You should receive a written report. It should include a checklist and note the condition of each item inspected.

For a Level 2, the video or photos of the flue give the report its evidence. A one-line note on an invoice isn't a report.

NFPA 211 doesn't dictate the report format. A good inspector provides one anyway.

### Using an Inspection Report for an Insurance Claim

A written report with video or photos can help document storm damage. Insurers often ask for documentation before approving a claim.

The report lists what was found and what to do about it. Some findings need repair. Others need a sweep or relining first.

### How Long Each Level Takes

Times vary by chimney. Roughly, a Level 1 with a sweep runs 45 to 60 minutes. A Level 2 with a sweep runs about 1.5 to 2 hours.

The video scan and the attic or crawlspace access account for the extra time.

### Inspection vs. Sweep

A sweep cleans the chimney. An inspection evaluates its condition.

They're different services, and they're often done in the same visit. See [chimney sweep](https://fortworthchimneysweep.com/chimney-sweep-fort-worth-tx/) for what a cleaning covers.

### How Often to Get Each Level

NFPA 211 calls for every chimney, fireplace, and vent to be inspected at least once a year. That annual inspection is normally a Level 1.

A Level 2 is event-driven, not scheduled. You get one when a trigger applies.

Even a chimney you never use needs the annual check. Animals nest in unused flues, and moisture damages liners regardless of use.

### NFPA 211, Building Codes, and Local Rules

NFPA 211 is a safety standard, not a law by itself. Building codes often reference it.

Local rules can differ. A good technician knows the codes that apply in your area.

### How to Prepare for Your Inspection

Clear the area around the fireplace or stove. Make attic and crawlspace access available.

Tell the technician about any changes or events, such as a new appliance, a storm, or a fire. Share earlier inspection reports if you have them.

### What to Say When Booking

Tell the company why you're calling. "Annual check, nothing changed" points to Level 1. "Buying a house" or "hail hit last week" points to Level 2.

Ask three questions. Which level is this? Does it include a video scan of the flue? Will I get a written report?

Also ask whether the technician is CSIA certified. CSIA Certified Chimney Sweeps are tested on all three inspection levels.
