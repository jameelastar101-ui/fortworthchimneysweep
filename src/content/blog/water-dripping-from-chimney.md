### What to Do Right Now

1. **Move furniture and belongings away from the drip.**
2. **Catch the water** with a bucket or towels.
3. **Photograph the drip, the stain, and any visible damage.**
4. **Stay off the roof.**
5. **Keep the fireplace unlit** until it's been checked.

If water is near a light fixture, outlet, or switch, don't touch it. Shut off power to that area at the breaker, if it's safe to reach.

A bulging or sagging ceiling can give way. Keep people out from under it.

### The Six Ways Water Gets In

- **Cap:** the metal cover over the flue opening. A missing or damaged cap lets rain fall straight in.
- **Crown:** the concrete slab at the top of the chimney. Cracks let water into the masonry.
- **Flashing:** the metal strips that seal the joint between the chimney and the roof. Gaps let water run down the chimney.
- **Porous masonry:** brick and mortar absorb water. Worn mortar joints let more in.
- **Chase cover:** the metal cover over the top of a prefabricated chimney's enclosure. It can rust or split at the seams.
- **Condensation:** water vapor that cools into liquid inside the flue, the passage that carries smoke out.

More than one source is common.

### Water Dripping Into the Firebox During Rain

Water falling into the firebox during rain usually means the flue is open at the top. A missing or damaged cap is the most common reason.

A rusted chase cover or a cracked crown can do the same. A closed damper may limit how much water reaches the room. It doesn't stop the leak.

### Stains and Drips on the Ceiling or Walls

Water follows gravity along the masonry, the framing, and the back of the firebox. It can travel several feet before it shows.

A ceiling stain rarely sits under the entry point. Flashing, the crown, and the roof around the chimney are the usual sources.

See [water stains on ceiling next to chimney](https://fortworthchimneysweep.com/water-stains-on-ceiling-next-to-chimney/) for what to check on the ceiling itself.

### Water Running Down the Outside or White Powder on the Brick

Brick and mortar are porous. They absorb rain like a sponge, and worn mortar joints let more in.

Water that freezes inside the masonry expands. That widens cracks and lets in more water.

White chalky powder on the brick is called efflorescence. It means water is moving through the masonry.

### Water in the Attic Beside the Chimney

The attic shows where water ends up. Damp insulation, dark stains, or wet wood beside the chimney point to the flashing or the roof deck, the plywood layer under the shingles.

Look from the attic hatch with a flashlight. Don't walk on the insulation or between the joists.

Photograph what you see. It helps the technician and any insurance claim.

### Water Pooling in the Ash Dump

Some fireplaces have an ash dump, a compartment below the firebox that collects ashes, often with a clean-out door near the base of the chimney.

Water there doesn't always come from above. It can seep in from the ground around the foundation.

If you find no stains or water tracks coming down from above, ground water is a possibility. A professional can tell the two apart.

### Condensation Inside the Flue

Condensation is water vapor turning to liquid on a cool surface. Burning gas produces water vapor.

In a flue that's oversized or poorly insulated, that vapor cools and condenses into water. Suspect condensation when water shows up without rain, especially with a gas appliance.

A camera check shows the liner and its size. A damp smell often comes with any moisture problem. See [fireplace smells wet](https://fortworthchimneysweep.com/fireplace-smell-wet/) for that symptom.

### Chimney Leak or Roof Leak?

Sometimes the chimney isn't the source. A roof leak higher up can run down the rafters and drip beside the fireplace.

A chimney professional checks the cap, crown, flashing, and masonry. A roofer checks the shingles and underlayment. See [how to repair a leaking chimney](https://fortworthchimneysweep.com/how-to-repair-a-leaking-chimney/) for how to tell which one applies.

### Gutters and Drainage Overloading the Chimney Base

Clogged gutters spill water against the chimney base and the flashing area. That can overload the seal there.

Keep the gutters near the chimney clear. Trim overhanging branches so debris doesn't pile up against the flashing.

### Timing Clues

The timing of the drip gives hints. These are general patterns, not proof.

- **Every time it rains:** a direct opening, such as a missing cap or a cracked crown.
- **Only in heavy or wind-driven rain:** flashing, the masonry, or a chase-top shroud catching blown rain.
- **Hours after the rain stops:** water stored in saturated masonry or framing, working its way down.

### Signs of a Serious Leak

- A musty smell near the fireplace.
- Orange rust on the damper or inside the firebox.
- Brown rings or stains on ceilings and walls around the chimney.
- Dripping sounds during or right after rain.
- Crumbling mortar or flaking brick.
- Peeling paint near the chimney.

Fuzzy growth means mold. See [mold in fireplace](https://fortworthchimneysweep.com/mold-in-fireplace/) for what to do.

### What Happens If the Water Keeps Coming

Water that keeps entering rots the roof deck, ceiling joists, and wall studs. Framing rot leads to sagging ceilings.

Moisture trapped behind drywall grows mold. The <a href="https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home" rel="nofollow" target="_blank">EPA's guide to mold, moisture, and your home</a> covers moisture control and mold basics.

### What You Can Check Yourself

From inside the house and the ground, you can look for stains, rust, and damp spots. You can look up the flue with a flashlight from the firebox. You can check the gutters near the chimney from the ground.

Don't go on the roof, and don't open the chimney enclosure. Don't poke at the flue.

Don't caulk the nearest visible gap. The gap you can see may not be the source, and sealing the wrong spot doesn't stop the water.

Sudden storm damage is more likely to be covered than gradual wear. Coverage depends on your policy.

Ask your insurer what documentation it needs. Photographs, the storm date, and an inspection report help.

### Preventing Drips

- Have the chimney inspected every year.
- Keep a properly fitted cap on the flue.
- Keep the crown and flashing in good condition.
- Keep gutters clear and branches trimmed back.
- Use only a vapor-permeable water repellent on brick. Paint and non-breathable sealers trap moisture inside the masonry.

The Chimney Safety Institute of America notes that water causes more damage to masonry chimneys than fire does. See its <a href="https://www.fireengineering.com/firefighter-training/water-causes-more-chimney-damage-than-fires/" rel="nofollow" target="_blank">water damage prevention tips</a>.
