## Foil vs rigid dryer duct at a glance

Use rigid metal duct for the main run and for every hidden section. Use only a short, exposed, UL 2158A-listed transition duct behind the dryer, and replace vinyl or plastic duct. Foil accordion duct depends on your dryer's manual and local code, and replacing it is the safer choice.

| Duct type | Main run | Inside walls, ceilings, or attic | Transition behind the dryer | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rigid metal | Yes | Yes | Too stiff to use | Best choice |
| Semi-rigid aluminum | No | No | Yes, if listed and short | Transition only |
| Flexible metal, UL 2158A listed | No | No | Yes, exposed and short | Transition only |
| Foil accordion | No | No | Only if your manual allows it and it is listed | Replace if unsure |
| Vinyl or plastic | No | No | No | Replace now |

The table reflects the rules commonly quoted for the International Residential Code. Your dryer's manual and local code decide the final answer.

## Why does dryer duct material matter?

Duct material matters because the inside surface decides how much lint sticks and how well the duct holds its shape. Smooth walls let air and lint pass through, while ridges trap lint and thin duct can sag or collapse.

Consumer Reports' testing engineer says <a href="https://www.consumerreports.org/appliances/clothes-dryers/how-to-prevent-dryer-fires-a6837216286/" rel="nofollow" target="_blank">plastic or foil accordion duct is risky</a> because it can sag, which lets lint build up at low points and in its ridges.

Duct makers also say solid metal holds a lint fire in better than thin foil.

## What are the five dryer duct types?

The five types are rigid metal, semi-rigid aluminum, flexible metal, foil accordion, and vinyl or plastic. Each one has a place, and only one belongs everywhere.

### Rigid metal duct

Rigid metal duct is smooth, solid pipe made of aluminum or galvanized steel. It belongs in the main run and in every hidden section.

It has no ridges to trap lint, and it keeps its 4-inch shape. Because it cannot bend, installers use elbow fittings for turns.

### Semi-rigid aluminum duct

Semi-rigid aluminum duct is corrugated metal that holds its shape after bending. It suits the short connection between the dryer and the wall duct.

According to duct suppliers, it has a smoother interior than foil and keeps its 4-inch opening. It can still crush, so do not push the dryer back against it.

### Flexible metal duct

Flexible metal duct is layered aluminum wound over wire, and some of it carries a UL 2158A listing. It is an exposed transition option only.

It should stay short and uncrushed.

### Foil accordion duct

Foil accordion duct is the thin, silver, ridged duct sold in many hardware stores. Whether it is acceptable depends on your dryer's manual and local code.

Structure Tech reports that the code asks only that a transition duct be UL 2158A listed. Kenmore tells owners to replace foil or plastic duct, and LG allows foil only when its manual names a listed type, according to <a href="https://structuretech.com/foil-dryer-ducts-code-approved/" rel="nofollow" target="_blank">Structure Tech's review of manufacturer manuals</a>.

Replace foil duct if you are unsure.

### Vinyl or plastic duct

White vinyl or plastic duct does not belong on a dryer. Most manufacturers and building codes prohibit it.

Replace vinyl duct before you use the dryer.

## How can you tell which duct you have?

Look at the color, the surface, and how the duct holds its shape. Use what you can see without moving the dryer farther than is safe.

- **Smooth, solid metal pipe with clamped or taped joints:** rigid metal.
- **Silver corrugated tube that keeps its bend:** semi-rigid aluminum.
- **Layered aluminum over wire that springs back:** flexible metal.
- **Thin, crinkly silver tube with deep accordion ridges:** foil.
- **White plastic tube with a ribbed spiral:** vinyl.

Look for a UL 2158A marking printed on the duct. Wear gloves, because semi-rigid foil-type duct has sharp edges.

## What does UL 2158A mean on a dryer duct?

UL 2158A marks a duct as listed for use as a clothes dryer transition duct. The listing matters only for the short connector behind the dryer.

As quoted for the 2024 International Residential Code, the transition duct must be one exposed length, listed and labeled to UL 2158A, and no longer than 8 feet. <a href="https://structuretech.com/dryer-duct-safety/" rel="nofollow" target="_blank">Structure Tech notes</a> that ducts carrying the listing are limited to that length.

The guide to [dryer vent length and routing](/dryer-vent-length-and-routing/) covers the length rules.

## Which joints and fasteners should you use on dryer duct?

Seal duct joints with foil tape or mastic and secure them with metal clamps. Do not use sheet-metal screws or cloth duct tape.

Screw tips stick into the duct and catch lint, and cloth duct tape dries out and fails. Structure Tech cites code language that calls for foil tape or mastic and rules out duct tape and screws.

Consumer Reports gives the same advice for homeowners: clamps or foil tape, not sheet-metal screws.

## Where does dryer duct crush or sag?

Dryer duct crushes behind the dryer and sags along long horizontal runs. A crushed or sagging duct narrows the airflow path, and lint collects at the narrow spot.

## What duct belongs in an attic or other hidden space?

Only rigid metal duct belongs in an attic, wall, floor, or ceiling. Foil and vinyl duct do not belong in hidden spaces, where a damaged section goes unseen.

A hidden duct is also harder to clean and inspect. The guide to [dryer vent length and routing](/dryer-vent-length-and-routing/) explains what to check on an attic run.

## How do you replace foil duct with rigid duct?

Replacing foil with rigid duct means swapping the main run for smooth metal pipe with elbow fittings and using a listed transition duct behind the dryer. Replacing concealed duct is a professional job.

A homeowner with an electric dryer can reasonably replace only the exposed transition duct. The guide to [DIY vs professional dryer vent cleaning](/diy-vs-professional-dryer-vent-cleaning/) explains where the DIY line sits.

A replacement typically involves these steps:

- Checking the run for length and bends.
- Installing rigid sections with elbow fittings.
- Sealing joints with foil tape and securing them with clamps.
- Connecting a listed transition duct.
- Testing airflow at the exterior hood.

Leave disconnecting and reconnecting a gas dryer to a professional. If you smell gas near the dryer, leave the house and call the gas company or 911.

## What drives the cost of replacing dryer duct?

Run length, bends, access, and the condition of the hood drive the cost of a replacement. Each one adds time or materials.

- **Run length:** longer runs need more duct.
- **Bends and fittings:** more turns need more elbows and sealing.
- **Access:** attic and wall runs take more time to reach.
- **Hood condition:** a damaged hood may need replacing too.

Ask what the quote covers and whether extra repairs are separate.

## Does duct material change how often to clean the vent?

Smooth rigid duct collects less lint than ridged duct, but every duct still needs cleaning. Material lowers the buildup and does not remove it.
